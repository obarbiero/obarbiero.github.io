title: Dealer Risk Limits and Currency Returns
subtitle: with F. Brauning, G. Joaquim, and H. Stein
journal: Conditionally Accepted at the Journal of Political Economy
filename: FXdealerlimits.pdf
blurb: We study how the risk limits of global dealer banks affect currency markets. We measure those limits directly using supervisory data. We find that tighter limits raise exchange rate volatility and move currency returns.
abstract: Currency markets are intermediated by a small number of large global banks, and their capacity to absorb flows driven by changing net demand should matter for the level and volatility of the exchange rate. To test this theory, we use a new dataset that directly measures the internal limits to risk-taking of major dealer banks. We provide two pieces of supportive evidence: (i) when the limits get tighter, the exchange rate becomes more volatile; (ii) when the limits get tighter on a bank holding an unusually long position in a given currency, that currency tends to depreciate.
